# Experiment 0032 — Switching was a curve, and everyone had tested a point

**Date:** 2026-09-01
**Result: a small amount of matchup-based switching beats the flat cost, confirmed on three independent seed sets (56.1%, 57.8%, 55.4%), and it ships.** The question had been answered wrongly three times because every previous attempt tested a single horizon. Swept on the fixed harness the curve is monotone and crosses even between horizons 2 and 4 — so 0004 and 0027 were both measuring a real effect at the wrong point. The optimal switch rate is **4.4%**, against the flat cost's 0.27% and rated humans' 11.8%. **Both ends were wrong.**

## The sweep

Fixed harness (0031), common random numbers, frozen pool. Reported as the paired sign test over matchups the two agents split, which is the right statistic once ties are deterministic.

```
mirror (control)     798 tied, none split     <- zero variance from anything but policy

horizon  2.0   paired 56.1%   p = 0.081
horizon  4.0   paired 50.2%   p = 0.947
horizon  6.0   paired 46.4%   p = 0.241
horizon  8.0   paired 42.9%   p = 0.016
horizon 12.0   paired 42.3%   p = 0.010
horizon 16.0   paired 39.6%   p = 0.000
```

Monotone, and it crosses 50% between 2 and 4. A single point anywhere above 4 says "switching is bad"; a single point at 2 says "switching is good". Both are true statements about the same curve.

## Confirmation, pre-registered

56.1% at p = 0.081 is one of six horizons and would not have survived a correction for that. So the rules were fixed before the run: fresh seeds (11, 23), horizons 1–3 chosen because the crossing sits between 2 and 4, and **one** claim — horizon 2 beats even.

```
horizon 1.0    53.5%   p = 0.325     switch rate 1.95%
horizon 2.0    57.8%   p = 0.013     switch rate 4.42%   <- the claim
horizon 3.0    57.3%   p = 0.011     switch rate 5.94%

flat cost (shipped)                  switch rate 0.27%
rated humans                         switch rate 11.80%
```

Then a third seed set (31, 47, 59) at 1,200 battles: **55.4%, p = 0.019**.

## The objection 0004 raised, finally tested

0004 rejected matchup switching on three signals. Human agreement and head-to-head have both been revisited since; the third — that it played *worse against Random*, 96.7% against 99.0% — had never been re-examined. A change that beats its peer while losing to a weaker opponent is usually exploiting something rather than playing better.

```
switching vs Random   1703/1704 = 99.9%
flat vs Random        1705/1705 = 100.0%
```

One matchup in 1,704. The objection does not reproduce.

## Why this took four attempts

Every ingredient had to be right at once, and each was wrong until now:

- **The pool.** 0024 found it was generated by a singles format, giving 8.5-turn battles. Switching cannot repay a turn that quickly.
- **The harness.** 0031 found `evaluate` never exchanged teams, leaving 93% of outcome variance uncontrolled and the confidence intervals too narrow.
- **Common random numbers.** Without them identical agents disagreed on 20% of matchups, all of it noise.
- **The shape.** Nobody swept. 0004 tuned the horizon to reproduce the human switch rate — which lands at roughly horizon 8, deep in the harmful region — and 0027 tested horizon 8 directly.

**Chasing the human rate was the specific error.** It is the fourth time following the corpus has been wrong (0010's Trick Room, 0013's targets, 0027's switching, now the calibration target itself), and the first time the right answer is known rather than merely "not that": 4.4%, less than half the human rate and sixteen times the agent's.

## What ships

`SWITCH_HORIZON = 2.0` and `matchup_switching` on by default. The flag stays so the flat cost is still constructible, and the flat scorer keeps its tests because it remains the baseline every switching measurement is made against.

## Not established

- Whether horizon 2 or 3 is better. They are 57.8% and 57.3% with overlapping intervals; 2 ships because it was the pre-registered value, not because it is measurably better.
- Whether the gain survives against opponents other than this heuristic and Random. 0026's blind spot applies: an improvement measured against a policy that shares our habits may not transfer.
- Whether the same "sweep it, do not sample it" treatment revives anything else. Tenure-priced setup, redirection and the richer evaluator were each tested at one or two settings on the old harness.
